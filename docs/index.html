Prototype · LLM agent on longitudinal health records

Statin Care-Gap Agent

An agent reads a patient's record, works out whether guidelines recommend a statin they are not taking, checks its own work, and hands uncertain cases to a clinician. A second agent talks with the patient to fill in what the record is missing.

The problem

Who should be on a statin and isn't

Many adults who meet guideline criteria for statin therapy are not on one. Finding them means reading each record for diagnoses, medications and stop dates, lipid panels and blood pressure within the last year, and then applying the 2018 AHA/ACC and USPSTF 2022 rules. That work is tedious for people and error-prone for a language model.

Users: primary care teams and population-health staff reviewing panels of patients. Output: one of six categories per patient — on statin, contraindicated, indicated (a care gap), consider, not indicated, insufficient data — with the facts and evidence behind it. An automated answer is only given when the system is confident and its facts pass a rule check.

How it works

One agent, a rule checker, repeated runs, and a clinician

Model: Qwen3-8B (open weights) served locally with vLLM. Data: Synthea synthetic records in DuckDB, coded with SNOMED, RxNorm and LOINC. The guideline arithmetic (pooled cohort equations) is a tool, not left to the model.

Limits and next steps

Limits

  • Synthetic records and simplified guideline rules. The labels measure faithful extraction and procedure, not clinical correctness.
  • The prompt was tuned on 30 development patients, who also fall in the formal samples (same sampling seed).
  • One model, one prompt. The simulated patient is an LLM and is more cooperative than real patients.
  • Safety comes at a cost: 22% of the random sample went to a clinician.

Failure types seen in transcripts

  • Understood but not recorded. The agent mentions intolerance in its summary and escalates, but never records the finding, so the decision does not change.
  • Evidence that does not support the value. Lab values copied from the record, with a quote saying no test was done (27 of 163 findings).
  • Stale data. Labs older than one year used as current, caught by the rule check.

Next steps

  • Budget-aware review order: choose which patients to review first under a token and clinician-time budget, using cost-aware Bayesian active learning.
  • Use the rule checker as a reward for fine-tuning (rejection-sampling SFT, then RL) so fewer cases need deferral.
  • Real de-identified records with free-text notes, and clinician review of deferred cases.
